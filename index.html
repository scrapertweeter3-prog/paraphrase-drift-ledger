Asking three ways is cheaper than being wrong once

A serving-cost audit usually starts with the token price list. The cheaper entry point is a paraphrase audit. Take the questions whose answers leave the building, the numbers headed into a deck or an invoice, and ask each one three ways the same day. When the three answers disagree on the load-bearing figure, that disagreement is the drift score, and it prices in dollars: fifteen hundred tokens in and five hundred out at list rates is a blended 1.2 cents a call, so forty questions a day costs 48 cents, $175.20 a year, and the three-way version of the same workload runs $1.44 a day, $525.60 a year. The extra $350.40 is the full price of knowing.

Run the ledger on the rework side. A wrong number that reaches a client deck costs a 45 minute apology call and a corrected send, about $45 of loaded time at $60 an hour. The full three-way audit breaks even at eight caught figures a year, a high bar for a small team. The targeted version audits the ten questions a day that carry numbers into client hands and single-shots the rest: sixty calls instead of forty, $262.80 a year, $87.60 over the baseline. One caught figure a quarter, $180 a year of avoided rework, clears that bar twice over.

The disagreement rate doubles as a maintenance light. A task class where every paraphrase set lands on the same figure for a month is stable, and the audit slots can rotate somewhere riskier. A set that splits two to one is a prompt problem wearing an answer costume: tighten the question, pin the units, and re-run the set. Dropping a paraphrase from a stable set cuts that question's audit bill by a third, so the protocol trims itself as the drift scores come in. The price list says what a call costs; only the re-ask says what an answer is worth.
